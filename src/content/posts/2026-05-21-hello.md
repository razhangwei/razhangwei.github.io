For a long time, I told myself I would start writing publicly once I had a conclusion worth publishing. That is backwards. Writing is often how a vague conclusion becomes a testable claim in the first place.

My day job is built around systems that learn from people. They are technical systems, but their important behavior lives at the boundaries: model and data, offline metric and user experience, paper result and operational constraint. Those boundaries are easy to mention and hard to reason about clearly. This site is an attempt to do more of that reasoning in public.

The two formats here serve different purposes:

- **Essays** are for ideas that have survived enough contact with reality to deserve a point of view.
- **Research notes** are for the useful intermediate state: a paper worth returning to, a question whose answer changed, or a distinction that I do not want to lose.

The scope is deliberately narrower than “everything AI.” I will mostly write about recommendation systems, multimodal models, representations, evaluation, and the engineering choices that decide whether a learning system remains useful as its world changes.

There will be gaps, revisions, and unfinished edges. That is part of the point. [RSS](/rss.xml) is the best way to follow along.
