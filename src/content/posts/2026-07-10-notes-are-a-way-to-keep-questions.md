The default way to “take notes on a paper” is to summarize it: task, method, metrics, conclusion. That record is often accurate and rarely memorable.

The note I want to keep is closer to a change log for my mental model. What did this paper make newly plausible? What assumption did it put under pressure? Which result would I expect to fail if the setting changed?

That leads to a small template:

1. **The claim:** what the work says, in my own words.
2. **The surprise:** the one detail that changed how I see the problem.
3. **The boundary:** the setting in which the result is likely to matter—and the setting where it may not transfer.
4. **The next question:** what I should look for, test, or read next.

This is not a substitute for careful review. It is an antidote to passive consumption. A useful research note should leave a thread behind that is easy to pick up later.
